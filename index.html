    How we can help you

    From emergency stroke care to long-term vascular conditions, every plan starts with a clear diagnosis and a clear explanation of your options.

    01Emergency stroke care

    Acute Ischemic Stroke

    Acute ischemic stroke occurs when a clot blocks blood supply to the brain, depriving cells of oxygen. If not treated promptly, this can cause irreversible brain damage, disability, or even death.

    02Neurovascular

    Arteriovenous Malformations

    Arteriovenous malformations are abnormal tangles of arteries and veins in the brain, disrupting normal circulation. They can remain silent but increase the risk of seizures, headaches, or life-threatening bleeding if ruptured.

    03Emergency stroke care

    Hemorrhagic Stroke

    Hemorrhagic stroke occurs when fragile blood vessels rupture, spilling blood into brain tissue. This sudden bleeding increases pressure inside the skull, damaging surrounding brain structures and impairing function.

    04Neurovascular

    Cerebral Aneurysm

    A cerebral aneurysm is a balloon-like bulge in a weakened artery wall within the brain. Though often symptomless, it poses a serious risk of rupture, leading to brain hemorrhage or stroke.

    Comprehensive Advanced Neurovascular Care

    01. Cerebral Angiogram (DSA)Precise Brain Vessel Imaging
    DSA is an advanced imaging procedure used to study blood vessels of the brain and spinal cord in real time. It helps identify aneurysms, blockages, and vascular malformations accurately.
    This detailed imaging guides correct diagnosis, treatment planning, and ensures safe execution of neurointerventional procedures.
    • Symptoms: Headache, neurological symptoms, suspected vascular disorders.
    • Causes: Abnormal blood vessels, blockages, aneurysms.
    • Benefits: Accurate diagnosis, precise planning, safer interventions.
    Read the full page →
    02. Brain Aneurysm TreatmentMinimally Invasive Aneurysm Treatment
    Aneurysm coiling is a minimally invasive procedure used to treat brain aneurysms by preventing rupture. It is performed when weakened blood vessels balloon and pose a serious risk of bleeding.
    Using thin catheters, soft coils are placed inside the aneurysm to block blood flow, reduce pressure, and safely prevent future bleeding.
    • Symptoms: Headaches, vision problems, weakness, seizures, sudden neurological deficits.
    • Causes: Vessel weakness, high blood pressure, smoking, genetics, aging.
    • Benefits: Prevents rupture, reduces bleeding risk, improves long-term brain outcomes.
    Read the full page →
    03. Flow Diverter StentsHealing Complex Brain Aneurysms
    Flow diverter treatment is a minimally invasive procedure used to treat complex brain aneurysms that are difficult to manage with surgery or coiling. It is commonly recommended for wide-neck or hard-to-reach aneurysms.
    A special device is placed inside the parent artery to redirect blood flow away from the aneurysm, allowing it to heal naturally while maintaining normal brain circulation.
    • Symptoms: Headaches, vision disturbances, neurological weakness, seizures, sudden deficits.
    • Causes: Vessel wall weakness, hypertension, smoking, genetics, aging.
    • Benefits: Promotes natural healing, reduces rupture risk, preserves circulation.
    Read the full page →
    04. Brain & Spinal AVMClosing Abnormal Brain and Spinal Vessels
    Brain and spinal AVMs are abnormal tangles of arteries and veins that bypass normal capillaries and can bleed, cause seizures, or damage nerve function.
    Embolization through catheters blocks the abnormal vessels with specialised agents, reducing bleeding risk and often preparing the lesion for surgery or radiosurgery.
    • Symptoms: Headaches, seizures, sudden bleeding, weakness, back pain, numbness.
    • Causes: Congenital vessel malformation; rarely acquired.
    • Benefits: Lower bleeding risk, protects brain and spinal cord, minimally invasive.
    05. Carotid StenosisPreventing Stroke Through Stenting
    Carotid and vertebral artery stenting is a minimally invasive procedure used to treat narrowing of arteries supplying blood to the brain, which significantly increases stroke risk. Symptoms may include dizziness, weakness, or transient vision loss.
    A stent is placed to widen the artery, restore smooth blood flow, prevent future strokes, and improve long-term brain circulation safely.
    • Symptoms: Dizziness, weakness, transient stroke symptoms.
    • Causes: Atherosclerosis, plaque buildup, aging.
    • Benefits: Prevents stroke, improves brain blood flow.
    Read the full page →
    06. Acute Stroke ThrombectomyRapid Stroke Clot Removal
    Mechanical thrombectomy is an emergency procedure for strokes caused by blocked brain arteries. It is performed when sudden weakness, speech difficulty, or paralysis occurs.
    Special catheters remove the clot quickly, restoring blood flow, minimizing brain damage, and significantly improving recovery when done within the treatment window.
    • Symptoms: Sudden weakness, speech difficulty, facial droop, paralysis.
    • Causes: Blood clots, atrial fibrillation, artery blockage.
    • Benefits: Restores circulation, limits brain damage, improves recovery.
    Read the full page →
    07. MMA Embolization (Subdural Haematoma)Preventing Recurrent Brain Bleeding
    MMA embolization treats chronic subdural hematoma caused by repeated bleeding from fragile membranes around the brain.
    By blocking the middle meningeal artery, the procedure reduces recurrence, supports healing, and may avoid repeat surgery.
    • Symptoms: Headache, confusion, weakness.
    • Causes: Minor head injury, aging, blood thinners.
    • Benefits: Reduces recurrence, supports recovery.
    Read the full page →
    08. Carotid-Cavernous FistulaClosing Abnormal Eye-Brain Shunts
    A carotid-cavernous fistula is an abnormal connection between a carotid artery and the cavernous sinus behind the eye, creating high-pressure venous flow. It may follow head injury or occur spontaneously.
    Endovascular embolization closes the connection with coils or liquid agents through catheters, relieving eye symptoms and protecting vision while preserving the carotid artery.
    • Symptoms: Red bulging eye, whooshing noise in the head, double vision, vision loss.
    • Causes: Head trauma, ruptured aneurysm, spontaneous vessel wall weakness.
    • Benefits: Vision protection, symptom relief, no open surgery.
    09. Vein of Galen MalformationTreating Rare Deep Brain Malformations
    Vein of Galen malformation is a rare congenital high-flow connection between brain arteries and a deep brain vein, often detected in newborns or infants.
    Staged embolization through catheters reduces abnormal flow, lowers strain on the heart and supports normal brain development.
    • Symptoms: Heart failure in newborns, enlarged head, seizures, developmental delay.
    • Causes: Congenital abnormality of vessel development.
    • Benefits: Heart relief, brain protection, minimally invasive for infants.
    10. Venous Sinus Stenting (IIH)Relieving Brain Venous Pressure
    Venous sinus stenting is used to treat narrowing of brain venous sinuses that leads to raised intracranial pressure, headaches, and vision problems.
    By restoring proper venous drainage, the procedure relieves pressure inside the brain and improves symptoms safely and effectively.
    • Symptoms: Severe headaches, vision problems, tinnitus.
    • Causes: Venous sinus narrowing or stenosis.
    • Benefits: Reduces pressure, improves symptoms.
    Read the full page →
    11. Venous Sinus Stenting (Tinnitus)Relief from Pulsatile Tinnitus
    Some patients hear a rhythmic whooshing sound in time with the heartbeat, caused by narrowing of a brain venous sinus.
    A stent opens the narrowed sinus, normalises venous flow and often reduces or removes the sound in carefully selected patients.
    • Symptoms: Pulsatile tinnitus, ear fullness, headache.
    • Causes: Transverse sinus stenosis, turbulent venous flow, sinus diverticulum.
    • Benefits: Symptom relief, minimally invasive, short recovery.
    12. Intracranial Artery StentingRestoring Brain Vessel Flow
    Intracranial stenting treats severe narrowing of arteries located within the brain that can cause recurrent strokes or reduced cerebral blood flow.
    The procedure widens the affected vessel, improves circulation, and lowers the risk of further ischemic events in carefully selected patients.
    • Symptoms: Recurrent strokes, weakness, speech difficulty.
    • Causes: Arterial narrowing, atherosclerosis.
    • Benefits: Improves flow, reduces stroke recurrence.

    13. Inferior Petrosal Sinus SamplingAccurate Hormone Source Detection
    Inferior petrosal sinus sampling is a diagnostic procedure used to identify the source of excess hormone production in pituitary disorders.
    By comparing hormone levels from specific veins, it helps accurately localize tumors and plan appropriate treatment.
    • Symptoms: Hormonal imbalance, weight gain, fatigue.
    • Causes: Pituitary gland disorders.
    • Benefits: Accurate diagnosis, targeted treatment.

    14. Intrasaccular Devices for Aneurysm (WEB, Contour)Direct Internal Aneurysm Sealing
    Intrasaccular devices are placed directly inside the aneurysm sac to block blood flow at its origin. They are useful for select wide-neck aneurysms.
    These devices seal the aneurysm internally without affecting the parent artery or surrounding brain circulation.
    • Symptoms: Headaches, vision changes, seizures, neurological weakness.
    • Causes: Vessel wall weakness, genetics, hypertension, smoking.
    • Benefits: Seals aneurysm safely, preserves parent artery flow.
    Read the full page →
    15. Spinal Vascular Malformations EmbolizationProtecting Spinal Cord Circulation
    Spinal vascular malformations interfere with normal spinal cord blood supply and may cause pain, weakness, or paralysis.
    Embolization seals abnormal spinal vessels, relieves symptoms, and prevents progressive neurological damage while preserving spinal cord function safely.
    • Symptoms: Back pain, weakness, numbness, walking difficulty.
    • Causes: Abnormal spinal blood vessel connections.
    • Benefits: Preserves spinal function, prevents paralysis.
    Read the full page →
    16. Cranial and Spinal Tumour EmbolizationReducing Tumor Blood Supply
    Tumour embolization is performed to reduce blood supply to highly vascular brain or spinal tumors prior to surgery.
    By minimizing tumor blood flow, it reduces surgical bleeding, improves safety, and enhances the effectiveness of tumor removal.
    • Symptoms: Headache, pain, weakness, neurological deficits.
    • Causes: Benign or malignant tumor growth.
    • Benefits: Safer surgery, reduced bleeding, improved outcomes.

    17. Intra-arterial Chemotherapy for RetinoblastomaTargeted Eye Cancer Therapy
    This treatment delivers chemotherapy drugs directly to eye tumors in children with retinoblastoma through targeted arteries.
    Direct delivery improves tumor control, preserves vision, and significantly reduces systemic side effects compared to conventional chemotherapy.
    • Symptoms: White eye reflex, vision loss, eye swelling.
    • Causes: Genetic mutation causing eye cancer.
    • Benefits: Preserves vision, reduces side effects, effective treatment.

    18. Thrombectomy for Resistant Venous Sinus ThrombosisRemoving Resistant Venous Clots
    This procedure is performed when blood clots in brain venous sinuses do not respond to anticoagulant medications.
    Mechanical thrombectomy removes the clot, restores venous blood flow, reduces brain swelling, and prevents serious neurological complications.
    • Symptoms: Headache, seizures, vision loss.
    • Causes: Blood clotting disorders, dehydration, infection.
    • Benefits: Prevents complications, improves recovery.

    19. Epistaxis and Skull Base Tumors EmbolizationControlling Severe Nasal Bleeding
    Embolization is used to treat severe, recurrent nosebleeds and bleeding skull base tumors that do not respond to conventional measures.
    Targeted blockage of abnormal blood vessels controls bleeding rapidly, reduces recurrence, and improves patient safety.
    • Symptoms: Recurrent nosebleeds, facial swelling.
    • Causes: Abnormal vessels, tumors.
    • Benefits: Rapid bleeding control, safer management.

    20. Balloon Test OcclusionAssessing Brain Blood Safety
    Balloon test occlusion evaluates whether the brain can safely tolerate permanent blockage of a major blood vessel.
    Temporary balloon inflation assesses collateral circulation, helping doctors prevent stroke during complex neurovascular treatments.
    • Symptoms: Pre-procedure assessment requirement.
    • Causes: Planned artery sacrifice.
    • Benefits: Ensures safety, prevents stroke.

    Vascular — Venous

    Focused Care for Venous Disorders

    01. IVC Filter PlacementProtection Against Pulmonary Embolism
    IVC filters are used in patients at high risk of pulmonary embolism when blood thinners are unsuitable or ineffective.
    The filter traps clots traveling from legs to lungs and can be safely removed later, reducing long-term complications.
    • Symptoms: High clot risk, recent DVT
    • Causes: Deep vein thrombosis, immobility
    • Benefits: PE prevention, temporary protection, minimally invasive
    02. Varicose Veins & DVTTreating Venous Reflux and Clots
    Varicose veins are enlarged, twisted veins caused by faulty valves. Deep vein thrombosis (DVT) is a clot in the deep veins that can cause swelling and pulmonary embolism.
    Endovenous ablation closes diseased veins with heat or glue, while catheter-directed thrombolysis or thrombectomy clears deep vein clots.
    • Symptoms: Visible bulging veins, leg ache, swelling, skin changes, ulcers.
    • Causes: Valve failure, prolonged standing, pregnancy, clotting tendency, immobility.
    • Benefits: Symptom relief, day-care treatment, rapid return to activity.
    03. Thrombectomy for Pulmonary EmbolismEmergency Removal of Lung Clots
    Pulmonary embolism occurs when large blood clots block lung arteries, causing sudden breathlessness, chest pain, and collapse.
    Thrombectomy removes the clot directly, restores blood flow, reduces strain on the heart, and saves life in high-risk patients.
    • Symptoms: Sudden breathlessness, chest pain, collapse
    • Causes: Deep vein thrombosis, immobility
    • Benefits: Rapid clot removal, lifesaving, heart protection
    04. Dialysis Fistula Thrombolysis / ThrombectomyRescuing a Clotted Dialysis Access
    A clot can suddenly block a dialysis fistula or graft and stop dialysis sessions.
    Catheter thrombolysis or mechanical thrombectomy removes the clot, and balloon angioplasty treats the underlying narrowing to restore the access.
    • Symptoms: No thrill or pulse in the access, failed dialysis, arm swelling, pain.
    • Causes: Vein narrowing, low flow, dehydration, clotting tendency.

    Specialised Treatments

    Acute Ischemic Stroke

    Acute ischemic stroke occurs when a clot blocks blood supply to the brain, depriving cells of oxygen. If not treated promptly, this can cause irreversible brain damage, disability, or even death.

    Timely diagnosis and treatments like clot-busting drugs or mechanical thrombectomy restore blood flow, prevent complications, and significantly improve recovery outcomes. Early recognition of symptoms is critical for saving brain function.

    Specialised Treatments

    Arteriovenous Malformations

    Arteriovenous malformations are abnormal tangles of arteries and veins in the brain, disrupting normal circulation. They can remain silent but increase the risk of seizures, headaches, or life-threatening bleeding if ruptured.

    Minimally invasive neurovascular techniques like embolization, radiosurgery, or surgery reduce risks and restore normal blood flow. Early detection and treatment are essential to prevent neurological complications and preserve brain health.

    Specialised Treatments

    Hemorrhagic Stroke

    Hemorrhagic stroke occurs when fragile blood vessels rupture, spilling blood into brain tissue. This sudden bleeding increases pressure inside the skull, damaging surrounding brain structures and impairing function.

    Immediate medical intervention focuses on controlling bleeding, reducing brain swelling, and preventing further complications. Surgery or endovascular care may be required, followed by rehabilitation to restore neurological functions and improve quality of life.

    Cerebral Aneurysm

    A cerebral aneurysm is a balloon-like bulge in a weakened artery wall within the brain. Though often symptomless, it poses a serious risk of rupture, leading to brain hemorrhage or stroke.

    Advanced treatments such as coiling or clipping help secure the aneurysm and prevent rupture. Regular monitoring, preventive care, and timely intervention reduce risks, ensuring better long-term brain health outcomes.

    Minimally Invasive Treatments for Improved Patient Health

    Mechanical Thrombectomy — Rapid Stroke Clot Removal. Mechanical thrombectomy is a minimally invasive procedure used to remove blood clots causing acute ischemic stroke. It is performed when a blocked artery prevents blood flow to the brain. The procedure restores circulation quickly, limits brain damage, and improves recovery when performed within the critical time window.

    Neurovascular Intensive Care — Advanced Brain Vessel Care. Neurovascular intensive care treats diseases of the brain and spinal blood vessels such as aneurysms, AVMs, and strokes using advanced catheter-based techniques. These minimally invasive procedures avoid open surgery, reduce complications, and support faster, safer recovery with precise treatment.

    Carotid/Vertebral Stenting — Preventing Stroke Risks. Carotid or vertebral stenting is a minimally invasive procedure performed to treat narrowing of arteries supplying blood to the brain, which increases the risk of stroke. A small stent is placed through a catheter to restore blood flow, reduce stroke risk, and avoid open surgery with faster recovery.

    Flow Diverter Device — Healing Complex Aneurysms. A flow diverter device is used to treat complex brain aneurysms that are difficult to manage with surgery or coiling, especially those with wide necks or challenging locations. It redirects blood flow away from the aneurysm, promotes natural healing over time, reduces rupture risk, and maintains normal circulation in surrounding brain vessels.

    Cerebral & Spinal Angiography — Precise Vessel Diagnosis. Cerebral and spinal angiography is a diagnostic imaging procedure used to study blood vessels in the brain and spine. It helps detect blockages, aneurysms, and abnormal vessels, enabling accurate diagnosis and safe treatment planning.

    Embolisation of dural AVF/AVM — Stopping Abnormal Blood Flow. Embolisation of dural AVF or AVM is done to block abnormal blood vessel connections that can cause bleeding or neurological symptoms. Special agents are used to seal abnormal vessels, protecting healthy brain tissue and preventing future complications.
